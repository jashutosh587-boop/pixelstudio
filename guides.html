Practical guides to better image files.

Use these short guides to understand dimensions, cropping, compression and formats before exporting your images.

1. Start with the final destination

Before resizing, check the dimensions and aspect ratio required by the website, app, form or print workflow where the image will be used. A larger pixel size does not automatically make an image look better if the original is small or soft. Avoid enlarging more than necessary, especially for detailed photographs.

2. Crop first, then resize

Choose the composition with the live crop frame. A fixed aspect ratio is useful when the destination asks for a known shape. Reposition the frame to keep the subject in view. Once the crop is applied, choose the required output dimensions. Cropping discards the pixels outside the selection, so keep the original file if you may want a different crop later.

3. Understand image formats

JPG uses lossy compression and is a practical choice for photos. PNG preserves lossless image data and transparency, making it useful for logos, interface graphics and screenshots. WebP supports lossy and lossless options and may produce compact files, but confirm that the destination accepts it. Converting a JPG to PNG does not restore details already lost to JPG compression.

4. Balance image quality and file size

For JPG and WebP, higher quality generally means a larger file. If you are preparing images for a web page, try a moderate quality setting, then inspect edges, text and fine patterns. Reduce pixel dimensions when the image will only appear as a small thumbnail. For archiving or print work, keep a full-size original.

5. Print dimensions and DPI

Physical dimensions and pixel dimensions are related by print resolution: pixels = millimetres ÷ 25.4 × DPI. For example, 35 × 45 mm at 300 DPI is approximately 413 × 531 pixels. This is a mathematical conversion, not a guarantee that the photograph meets an authority's requirements. Check the current official specifications, including face size, background, lighting, expression and recency.

6. Remove a plain background

Color-key background removal works when the background has a reasonably even color that is different from the subject. Sample the background with the corner sampler or by clicking a point in the preview. Increase tolerance to remove more similar shades and use edge softness to reduce harsh boundaries. If subject and background colors overlap, the tool may remove parts of the subject. Complex scenes usually need a dedicated segmentation editor and manual review.

7. Add a useful watermark

Use a short brand name, copyright notice or signature. Keep it visible but not so large that it obscures the image. A contrasting color and moderate opacity can help. A watermark is not a substitute for retaining source files or managing image licensing.

Frequently asked questions

Are photos uploaded?
The editor processes selected image files locally in the browser and does not send photos to a PixelCraft image-processing server.

Can I convert every image format?
JPG, PNG and WebP are supported in the supplied tools. Browser support for less common formats varies; GIF files may be processed as a still frame depending on how the browser decodes them.

Can the passport tool guarantee acceptance?
No. It sets print dimensions and creates a centered crop, but it does not validate any government or visa photo rules. Check the receiving authority's current instructions.
